I spent 75 minutes with the Women in Leadership class at CU Boulder's Leeds School of Business, a mix of business and engineering students from the [Business-Engineering Women in Technology (BEWiT)](https://www.colorado.edu/business/undergraduate-programs/women-business/business-engineering-women-technology) program, working through the networking ideas they'd studied that week in Herminia Ibarra and Mark Hunter's _How Leaders Create and Use Networks_ and Ibarra's [Building Effective Networks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k115ePA_9SU).

As first-year students, most don't have networks yet in the traditional career sense. I wanted to leave them with something actionable to get started, not just tell them to go build one, so the whole session ran on a reframe: **your network is the people you already know or people you want to meet.**

We started with my story entering tech through a nontraditional path (non-profits, marketing, support, then software engineering) and how the network followed during each pivot. Then the students worked from the three reach-outs:

1. Who haven't I thanked lately, who went out of their way for me?
2. Who in my work or school life could I get to know better?
3. Who in my personal life could I learn more about what they do?

For each, they wrote a name on the list and ideas for the first line they'd actually send, then chose the one they'd send that week. We closed with a live networking practice because this was one of their first few classes. Most hadn't even met yet!

The whole point: they left with a short list of concrete, low-stakes reach-outs. The act of reaching out _is_ the networking, and it doesn't require an event. It can be small and more meaningful than it appears on the surface.
