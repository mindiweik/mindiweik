I spent 75 minutes with the Women in Leadership class at CU Boulder's Leeds School of Business, a mix of business and engineering students from the [Business-Engineering Women in Technology (BeWiT)](https://www.colorado.edu/business/undergraduate-programs/women-business/business-engineering-women-technology) program, working through the networking ideas they'd studied that week in Herminia Ibarra and Mark Hunter's _How Leaders Create and Use Networks_ and Ibarra's [Building Effective Networks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k115ePA_9SU).

Most of the students were first-years, and they don't have networks yet in the career sense. I did not want to hand them a blank three-circle diagram and tell them to go build one, so the whole session ran on a reframe: **your network is the people you already know, you just haven't reached out to them yet.**

I told one story about entering tech through a nontraditional path (non-profits, marketing, support, then software engineering) and how the network followed me rather than staying behind with the field. Then the students worked from the three reach-outs:

1. Who haven't I thanked lately, who went out of their way for me?
2. Who in my work or school life could I get to know better?
3. Who in my personal life could I learn more about what they do?

For each, they wrote a real name and the first line they'd actually send, then circled the one they'd send that week. We closed with a live intro brokering bit, connecting students in the room to people they'd just realized they wanted to meet.

The whole point: they left with three concrete, low-stakes reach-outs instead of an abstract diagram. The act of reaching out _is_ the networking, and it doesn't require an event.
